## Round 1 Initial draft

Deep Review document: cfc7fbd2-e25a-4205-9cea-2a09f614aa9b. Input: SELF_X_IEEE_Paper.pdf (original nine-page draft). Output after feedback: SELF_X_Revision_1.pdf. The next local revision adds a seed-level sensitivity analysis and a diagnostic control for the repeated governor output.

1. **Standalone DPP and stage attribution.** Added the 100 ms DPP-only arm with initial V=8, initial weights and the shared guard; compared the matched 5 s stack and removed each language model layer separately. S5 is 0.12 ms slower than DPP alone (Holm-adjusted p=0.0745). The study claims no advantage over fast DPP. Sections IV–VI and both results figures.
2. **Loop interfaces and startup.** S3 applies the adapter proposal after four slots. S4 and S5 use it as a bounded preference in the score. S4 chooses every 5 s; S5 every slot. The governor changes V and weights after eight slots and cannot change the action set. Both components keep their previous state while advice is pending. Sections IV–V.
3. **Drift units, clipping, capacity notation, and backlog theorem.** Replaced the earlier theorem with the actual rate-form score. Its queue term has units of Mbit squared per second; multiplication by slot duration gives the linear term in unclipped quadratic drift. The finite buffer caps occupancy by construction. Neither the guard nor clipping proves stability or a drop guarantee. Section IV-A.
4. **Buffer saturation example.** Stated the constant excess arrival rate explicitly. An empty 2 Mbit queue fills in 200 ms at 20 Mbps of arrivals and 10 Mbps of service. Section I.
5. **Loss floor.** Removed the 7.97% claim and associated rounding/plot discrepancies. Buffer space can absorb instantaneous excess. The revised study reports metered and overflow volumes without treating a bufferless quantity as unavoidable loss. Sections III and VI.
6. **Action-to-rate mapping, metering and cost.** Defined per-path admitted rates, meter drops, clipping, overflow, served volume, and all five score terms. Both meter synthesis rules and the guard edge cases are explicit. Negative weights are clipped before renormalization, and V is bounded. Sections III–IV.
7. **Delay and rule changes.** Replaced Little's Law wording with a virtual waiting time proxy at service rate. Defined the clipped slot integral, served-volume weighting, and the difference between a window rule-change count and the slot indicator used to train neural baselines. Sections III and V.
8. **Telemetry and adapter provenance.** DPP uses exact simulator state. The action prompt rounds demand to half-Mbps; governor and critique prompts round demand and queues to integers. The paper documents the offline grid, 24 distinct states (13 training, 11 gate), gate seeds and labeling rule. Sections IV–V and the checkpoint metadata.
9. **Window history.** Only S2 appends its last effective action and realized reward after each completed five-second window. The governor does not append an epoch replay record. No arm retrains the shipped adapter during the test. Sections IV–V.
10. **Latency, quota, replay and units.** Replaced the earlier provider experiment with real local inference and an assumed 400 ms per sequential stage, including cache hits; specialists share one stage. Removed remote quota, pacing, fallback, 331 ms, and speedup claims because this experiment measures none of them. This also removes the fallback-chain grammar issue. Sections IV–V.
11. **Sample and statistics.** Stated 14 configurations × 12 paired traces × 1,800 slots = 302,400 test slots. Inference uses 12 paired trace differences with equal weight for each profile, pointwise t intervals, and 13 Holm-adjusted tests. A descriptive 20,000-draw bootstrap resamples whole paired traces within profile. All comparator effects are archived. Section V-B and comparison data.
12. **Online tabular baseline asymmetry.** Labeled Tabular Q as a cold-start online reference and reported its exploration schedule; added separate offline-trained frozen DQN and IQL comparisons. A frozen tabular variant was not added, and no conclusion about converged tabular Q is made. Section V-A.
13. **Novelty and negative LoRA results.** Positioned the work against agentic SDN, NetLLM, two-timescale governance and reflection-based slicing. The paper makes no mechanism novelty claim. It reports the weaker direct LoRA arm and the paired result of removing its prior from S5. Sections II and VI.
14. **Zero-shot transfer and profile names.** Removed the unmatched 73% transfer claim and vague cross-reference. The revised study compares all controllers on the same three synthetic test profiles and reports cadence effects by profile. It does not label any profile as unseen. Sections V–VII.
15. **Figures, rounding and notation.** Replaced the historical plots with vector figures and a table from the audited data. This removes overlapping provider, capacity and reward labels; integer delay bars; inconsistent loss-floor rounding; the bound legend; and inconsistent delta-policy notation. Sections IV and VI.

The first review's citation checker verified all 12 paper references it checked in the original draft and skipped one non-paper reference. This does not certify the bibliography of a later revision.

## Round 2 Revision 1

Deep Review document: edb928d1-957f-4f28-9c8e-a6bed8513777. Input: SELF_X_Revision_1.pdf. Completed. Output after feedback: SELF_X_Revision_2.pdf. The two review exports are included as separate files.

1. **Direction and component interaction.** The abstract now says S5 was 0.12 ms slower than DPP alone and that the difference did not survive Holm adjustment. It also states that the prior increased delay in the governed stack. Section VI gives the 0.49 ms descriptive interaction and explains why reducing V makes a fixed prior margin larger relative to the penalty term. The first update actually applies at 0.8 s; V reaches 0.5 at 90.8 s. The ablation does not separate V from concurrent reweighting and state changes.
2. **Novelty, model capability and external validity.** Added NetLLM and the agentic SDN reference and positioned the work as an experimental comparison. Section VII limits claims to one model, prompt design and synthetic fluid topology; the study cannot identify model size as the cause of repeated outputs. No hardware or packet-level claims were added.
3. **Current-slot information and objective fairness.** Sections IV–V distinguish access to exact current demand and the deterministic transition model from access to future demand. The neural arms see the same current demand but lack the DPP counterfactual transition calculation. The text now compares the learning reward, 2.5-per-switch penalty, and 1.5 loss coefficient with DPP's distinct cost and action proxies. Reported trade-offs concern the configured policies.
4. **Preference feasibility and initialization.** The preference is rechecked on each DPP decision; an infeasible preference is ignored, not converted to primary. The first four fast slots have no preference. Section IV.
5. **Prompt inputs, governor math and notation.** The direct prompts use the same half-Mbps rate representation, capacity and flags. Specialist prompts use integer-rounded current rates and queues; the orchestrator additionally sees the ballots. Neither sees past performance or objective weights. Added explicit clipping and renormalization equations, named the update tuple, and made decimals and metering subscripts consistent. Sections III–IV.
6. **Baseline interface and meter rules.** Specified the threshold and tabular meter formula, floor interaction and bulk-rate rejection example. Clarified least-loaded's two-action menu, the exact tabular buckets and exploration, neural normalization, and IQL binary votes, precedence and replay. Split Table I into fourteen aligned rows. Sections IV–V.
7. **Timing and guard holds.** Stated that S2 adds 0.4 s without a delay-matched S1 control; held proposals keep their numeric meter caps; per-slot vetoes do not latch. S4 consumes advice at the next window boundary, while S5 can apply it when ready. Sections IV–V.
8. **Synthetic traffic and metric weighting.** Added the flash-crowd parameters, including how the generator tests its event clock, and the volatile rate formula. The delay estimand gives windows equal weight. The accompanying analysis adds episode-wide volume-weighted delay and terminal backlogs derived from conservation. Treating terminal backlog as undelivered leaves the S5–DQN/IQL gap at 0.33 percentage points at the reported precision. Section V.
9. **Shared guard and matching neural actions.** Instrumented replay retrained the neural baselines and matched their training fingerprints, windows and action hashes. The policies disagreed in 891 of 21,600 proposals; every disagreement involved a veto and both applied actions were primary. S1/S2 also applied no meters; identical loss and goodput do not imply identical slot actions. Section VI and neural_guard_check.json.
10. **Complete paired effects and numerical precision.** Added the effect, pointwise interval, raw p and Holm p for every comparator. Kept both distinct neural policies in the primary family and verified that collapsing their identical realized delays leaves the 5% decisions unchanged. The paper now uses three decimal places for S5 goodput and explains why unrounded paired effects differ from subtracting rounded means. Added leader lines and identified all five plotted configurations. Section VI.
11. **Fixed governor control.** With the same simulated timing and LoRA prior, a fixed governor choice reproduced all 432 S5 windows and all 12 action hashes. It was selected after observing the invariant outputs, so this is a diagnostic and not a prospective baseline. Section VI and fixed_governor_check.json.
